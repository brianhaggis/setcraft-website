What's New in Setcraft

August 4, 2026 Current

Version 1.5.2
  • Bandsintown can now check for new shows on its own. A per-artist checkbox in Backstage → Integrations, "Check for new shows when Setcraft opens," imports any new Bandsintown dates every time the app launches. A small message reports how many arrived; when there is nothing new, nothing appears. If a check fails, the outcome shows only in Backstage next to the Bandsintown fields, along with the last-checked time. Off by default, and part of the paid version and trial like manual sync.
  • PRO reports now carry the watermark on unpaid installs, matching every other printed sheet (the free tier and the trial; paid output stays clean).
  • Deleting a setlist now also removes its show's tech cue sheet, so stale FOH and lighting notes can no longer resurface on the next setlist built for that show.
  • Replacing a chart can no longer destroy the old one. The new file is written completely before the old file is removed, so a failed upload (full disk, permissions) leaves the existing chart in place.
  • Restores refuse to run without their safety net. Both the portable backup restore and the snapshot rollback now stop, with your data untouched, if the automatic pre-restore snapshot cannot be saved.
  • Rolling back to an old snapshot no longer empties newer parts of the database. Tables added by an app update after the snapshot was taken are left as they are instead of being wiped.

August 1, 2026

Version 1.5.1
  • Databases now repair themselves on launch. Two long-standing migration bugs are fixed: an install whose preferences table held duplicate rows now merges them cleanly (the old repair failed silently every time), and an interrupted photo-counter rebuild no longer leaves the database wedged in a state it could never recover from. Both repairs were verified against deliberately broken databases.
  • Setlist photos taken sideways now import right side up. Phone cameras store portrait shots as rotated data plus an orientation tag. The analyzer now honors the tag, so a two-column setlist photographed in portrait keeps its sets in the right order instead of swapping them.
  • Onboarding polish. The setup wizard's theme previews now show each theme's real artwork instead of a flat color. Welcome dialogs wait until a tutorial tour finishes instead of covering it. The tour dims the page less. The sample band's songs no longer trigger achievement celebrations on a brand-new install.
  • The free version and trial watermark is now described accurately in the app as a watermark overlay across each page.

July 25, 2026

Version 1.5.0
  • Setcraft now runs on Intel Macs. There are two downloads: one for Apple Silicon (M1 and later) and one for Intel. The download page picks the right one for you, and the file names say which is which. If you move to a new Mac, get the download that matches it; your library and license carry over the same way they always have.
  • Fixed: the app would not open on macOS 12, 13 or 14. Setcraft has always said it needs macOS 12 or newer, and that was wrong. Every version up to 1.4.18 actually needed macOS 15. On an older system it would install, look fine, and then fail to open, with no useful explanation. 1.5.0 genuinely runs on macOS 12 and up, which is what the app claimed all along. If a bandmate ever told you it just would not start, this is why, and this is the fix.
  • Printed setlists are unchanged. Every part of the printing engine was replaced underneath. Setlists, cue sheets and PRO reports were compared page by page before and after, on real shows, and they come out identical, down to the pixel. Intel and Apple Silicon print identically too.
  • Fixed a font-handling fault that could have caused the app to close unexpectedly on an Intel Mac while making a PDF.

July 13, 2026

Version 1.4.18
  • The builder's City highlight now casts a real net. Shows within 50 miles count as the same market, so a night in Ypsilanti lights up your Ann Arbor history. City lookups run against an offline city map bundled with the app; nothing leaves your Mac. Same-named cities in different states no longer collide, and a same-named venue in another city no longer matches.
  • The history bar now tells you when you were last there. "Last at The Ark · 120 days ago" in Venue mode; in City mode it says "Last in" or "Last near" and names the neighboring town when that's where the history came from.
  • Highlighted library songs now carry a "played here" tag (or "played nearby" in City mode), so the highlight explains itself.
  • The freshness meter reads correctly in City mode. It now compares tonight against the same songs the highlight marks, instead of hiding when the rest of town had no history.
  • Printed setlists name the setlist's band. Printing another of your bands' setlists stamped the active profile's name across the top; the sheet now belongs to the band that played it.
  • Nav switch show/hide preferences now survive relaunches.
